Portfolio for the MFA in Theatre Arts, Design and Technology

I design experiences by learning how places feel.

Human geographer, program designer and creative placemaker. I turn the textures, sounds and stories of a place into worlds that people can walk through.

From field scouting to world-building

Theme-park designers scout a place before they build a world. I have spent years doing the same for real cities, then designing the experience that follows.

At the National Defense University I designed and led place-based field practicums across the United States for groups of 100+ participants, managing budgets over $500k. Through Lost Pilgrim, my own practice, I carry that method into community workshops that blend GIS, storytelling, art and making. I am applying to theatre design and technology because I want to bring the same research-driven, sensory approach to the stage, and to add the craft, collaboration and technical depth that theatre teaches.

My background in creative writing and languages, my visual art, and my fabrication work (3D modeling, resin printing, welding, animation) all feed one question: how does a designed space change what people feel, remember and do?

How do you teach a region by letting people meet it?

Concept. For each region, I immersed myself in its character first, then built a storyline from the people and places that carry it. Each practicum is sequenced like a piece of theatre: contrast, pacing, and moments of quiet between moments of intensity.

What does it take to invent a world you could step into?

Memory Maps and Microseasons Lost Pilgrim community projects, San Diego
Role
Concept, design, fabrication, facilitation
Methods
Field journaling, participatory mapping, large-scale physical map

What does a neighborhood remember, and how does a year really change?

Memory Maps. My original idea of memory topography: residents map their memories onto local places, revealing shared stories, beloved landmarks and the overlooked character of a neighborhood. I built a very large interactive map that residents could add to by hand.

Microseasons. Inspired by traditional Chinese microseasons, the community collectively made a San Diego calendar of subtle seasonal changes through observation and field journaling.
